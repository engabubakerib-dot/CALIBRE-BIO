## 1. Research question

Can a layered, constraint-based verification architecture, coupled with formally
guaranteed uncertainty calibration through conformal prediction, substantially reduce
biologically invalid LLM outputs **without suppressing scientifically novel ones**, across
heterogeneous biological claim types and multiple model families?

Secondary questions:

- **SQ1** Which verification layers contribute how much reduction, and where does
  hallucination concentrate qualitatively in each domain?
- **SQ2** Do calibrated risk scores retain reliable statistical coverage out of distribution?
- **SQ3** Does novelty-aware triage preserve novel-but-valid outputs better than a
  distribution-based filter at a matched false-acceptance budget?

## 2. Design

Within-claim comparison of filtering conditions over a fixed bank of model-generated,
expert-adjudicated biological claims. Each condition is a function from a claim to
{accept, flag, reject}; all conditions see the identical claim bank, so differences are
attributable to the filter alone. Pilot domains: AMR and small-molecule design. Expansion
to protein function and clinical variant interpretation is conditional on the
month-6 milestone (§9) and will be registered as an addendum.

## 3. Hypotheses and decision thresholds

Primary outcome: **ICR** (invalid claim rate among accepted claims).

| | hypothesis | threshold for support |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | The full CALIBRE stack reduces ICR relative to the raw condition | relative ICR reduction **≥ 50%**, and the 95% bootstrap CI for the reduction excludes 0 |
| **H2** | Conformal risk scores retain nominal coverage | empirical coverage within **90% ± 3 points** per pre-declared stratum, in-distribution **and** on the OOD panel |
| **H3** | Novelty-aware triage preserves novel-but-valid claims better than a distribution-based filter | **NRR ≥ 2×** the distribution-filter NRR at matched FAR (±1 point) |
| **H4** | The effect generalises across model families | spread of per-family relative ICR reduction **≤ 10 points** |

Each hypothesis is evaluated once, on the full bank, after the pilot phase closes.
A hypothesis whose threshold is not met is reported as not supported; thresholds are not
revised post hoc.

## 4. Metric definitions (implemented in `src/calibre/evaluation/metrics.py`)

Let claims be labelled `valid`, `invalid`, `novel_plausible` or `unresolvable` (§6).
`unresolvable` claims are excluded from all primary metrics; their rate is reported.

- **ICR** = (invalid ∧ accepted) / accepted — *primary*
- **FAR** = (invalid ∧ accepted) / invalid — the matched budget in H3
- **NRR** = (novel_plausible ∧ not rejected) / novel_plausible
- **VRR** = (valid ∧ accepted) / valid
- **flag rate** = flagged / all — the human-review cost
- **coverage** = fraction of claims whose conformal label set contains the true label

Primary analysis counts a flagged claim as **not accepted** (a human reviews it). A
secondary worst-case analysis counts flagged as accepted; both are reported for every
condition, because a firewall that merely moves errors into an unread review queue has not
reduced risk.

## 5. Conditions

1. `raw` — model output unfiltered (accept all). Defines the baseline ICR.
2. `self_consistency` — k = `FILL-ME` samples, majority agreement threshold.
3. `retrieval_only` — retrieval-augmented generation, no constraint layers.
4. `distribution_filter` — exponential distributional filter (operationalising Rathkopf's
   proposal) at matched FAR; the comparator for H3.
5. `calibre_full` — L1+L2+L3+L4+L5.
6. Ablations — L1; L1+L2; L1+L2+L3; L1+L2+L3+L4; and leave-one-layer-out of the full stack.

## 6. Materials

**Claim bank.** Target n = `FILL-ME` adjudicated claims (pilot: ≥ 200 across the two
pilot domains; final bank size fixed before registration). Claims are generated by the
pinned models from a fixed prompt set (`data/prompts/`), decomposed into atomic claims,
and labelled per `docs/claim_taxonomy.md`.

**Annotation.** Two independent annotators label every claim. Pre-declared agreement
target **Cohen's κ ≥ 0.70** on `gold_label` before the bank is scaled beyond the pilot;
κ is reported for the final bank. Disagreements are adjudicated by a third annotator, and
the adjudicated label is used. A pre-declared random subsample of n = `FILL-ME` claims is
re-annotated by both annotators for the label-noise sensitivity analysis (§8) — this is
the mitigation for the only risk graded *high* in the project risk table (incompleteness
of curated databases).

**Evidence sources, pinned by release** (recorded in `configs/databases.toml` and
`data/evidence_versions/`): CARD, ResFinder, UniProtKB, NCBI Gene/PubMed, ChEBI, GO,
ClinVar. Every layer verdict records the release it was produced against.

**Models, pinned by dated snapshot** (`configs/models.toml`): one snapshot per family,
≥ 4 families, including ≥ 2 open-weight models served locally via vLLM at a fixed
Hugging Face revision. Floating aliases (`gpt-4o`, `deepseek-chat`) are not admissible as
pinned snapshots. All generation for a reported experiment is completed within one
declared window and cached to disk, so later retirement of a hosted snapshot does not
invalidate the results.

## 7. Calibration procedure (L4)

Split conformal prediction with a finite-sample corrected quantile
(⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th smallest nonconformity score), α = 0.10. Mondrian (per-stratum)
calibration over pre-declared strata: domain × claim type × difficulty. Calibration and
test splits are disjoint at the **prompt** level, so claims from one prompt never appear on
both sides. Risk scores combine semantic entropy over k sampled answers (Farquhar et al.,
*Nature* 2024) with L1–L3 verdict features; the exact feature list is fixed before
registration. The OOD panel for H2 is `FILL-ME` (declared in advance, held out entirely).

## 8. Analysis plan

- Point estimates with **95% percentile bootstrap CIs**, resampling at the **prompt**
  level (claims from one prompt are correlated), b = 2000, seed recorded.
- H1: relative ICR reduction, raw vs `calibre_full`.
- H2: coverage per stratum, in-distribution and OOD, against the 0.90 ± 0.03 band.
- H3: NRR at matched FAR (±1 point), `calibre_full` vs `distribution_filter`.
- H4: per-family relative ICR reduction; reported spread against the 10-point threshold.
- Multiplicity: the four hypotheses are tested once each on pre-declared primary metrics;
  per-layer and per-domain breakdowns are **exploratory** and labelled as such.
- Sensitivity analyses: (a) flagged counted as accepted; (b) hedged claims excluded;
  (c) label-noise simulation from the double-annotated subsample; (d) `unresolvable`
  claims counted as invalid (worst case).
- Missing data: a layer returning `unknown` is not imputed; it propagates to `flag`.

## 9. Timeline and stopping rule

| phase | months | milestone |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | week 1 | registration; snapshots pinned; pilot domains fixed |
| 1 | 1–3 | infrastructure; gold bank (100–200 claims, pilot domains); κ check |
| 2 | 2–6 | L1→L5 implemented; **month-6 gate**: pilot-domain results + preprint |
| 3 | 7–9 | full CALIBRE-Bench; public claim bank; multi-family evaluation |
| 4 | 10–12 | ablations, OOD panels, v1.0 open-source release |
| 5 | 13–18 | manuscript (*Briefings in Bioinformatics* first), deposition, review |

**Month-6 gate:** expansion to the protein and variant domains proceeds only if the pilot
reaches κ ≥ 0.70 and a measurable ICR reduction in both pilot domains. If not, the scope
stays at two domains and the paper reports that.

## 10. Deviations and transparency

Any deviation from this protocol is recorded in `docs/deviations.md` with its date, reason
and the analysis affected, and reported in the manuscript. Code, prompts, claim bank, layer
outputs and call logs are released at submission; raw API call logs (`experiments/logs/`)
are included so that every verdict can be traced to its model snapshot and database release.
